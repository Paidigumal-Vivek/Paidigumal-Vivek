# Findings & Operational Considerations

## Executive assessment

The CAD analysis converted approximately 71,864 incident records into a three-page operational intelligence dashboard. The resulting view identifies where workload is concentrated, when demand increases, which incident categories drive activity, where repeat service demand occurs, and how response/deployment patterns vary.

## Required baseline measures

### 1. Incident type assessment

**Finding:** Traffic Stops represented the highest service demand across the reporting period. Other high-frequency categories included Noise Complaints, Harassing Phone Calls, and Welfare Checks.

**Operational relevance:** Incident composition can inform patrol deployment, recurring-service analysis, proactive initiatives, and specialized response considerations.

### 2. Township workload assessment

**Finding:** Plainfield Township demonstrated the highest workload among the jurisdictions represented in the dataset.

**Operational relevance:** Workload concentration can be used as a starting point for reviewing whether staffing and patrol deployment align with observed demand.

### 3. Temporal workload assessment

**Finding:** Call activity increased after 7:00 AM and peaked during the afternoon and evening, particularly around 2:00 PM–8:00 PM. Overnight activity was comparatively lower.

**Operational relevance:** Demand cycles provide an evidence base for reviewing staffing schedules and patrol coverage rather than assuming uniform demand throughout the day.

### 4. High-frequency location assessment

**Finding:** 7,192 repeat addresses were identified, indicating locations with recurring CAD activity.

**Operational relevance:** Repeated service locations can be candidates for continued monitoring, directed patrol, and longer-term problem-solving analysis.

### Priority

Priority 5 represented the dominant workload category in the analysis. This provides command staff with a clearer picture of the urgency mix within overall CAD activity.

### Call source

Land Line, Field Initiated, and 911 were among the dominant call sources. Understanding intake channels can help contextualize how service demand enters the CAD system.

### Response performance

Most recorded incidents fell within the shorter response-time categories. The dashboard also retained a Missing response-time category so incomplete timestamps remained visible for operational review.

### Deployment

Most incidents were associated with one assigned unit. Unit-assignment grouping provides an additional lens for evaluating deployment intensity and identifying incidents that routinely require multiple resources.

### Township response comparison

Average response time by township was included to highlight jurisdictions that may warrant operational review. This comparison should be treated as a screening indicator rather than a causal conclusion because response time can be affected by factors not present in the dataset.

### Geographic intelligence

Latitude/longitude were used to visualize the spatial distribution of CAD activity. The geographic page supports township filtering, cross-filtering, unit-deployment visualization, and localized investigation of operational patterns.

## Recommended leadership considerations

1. Evaluate patrol deployment during afternoon/evening peak demand.
2. Review workload concentration within Plainfield Township for resource alignment.
3. Monitor recurring service locations for opportunities for proactive problem solving.
4. Examine townships with comparatively longer average response times.
5. Use interactive filtering to support recurring command-level operational reviews.

## Interpretation guardrails

These findings identify patterns and operational questions; they do **not** establish causation. Staffing levels, geographic travel conditions, incident outcomes, concurrent calls, and other contextual variables were not part of the provided analytical frame. Any operational decision should therefore combine this dashboard with agency context and additional intelligence.
